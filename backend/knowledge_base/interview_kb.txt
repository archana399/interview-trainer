BEHAVIORAL INTERVIEW: STAR METHOD
The STAR method is the gold standard for answering behavioral interview questions.
STAR stands for: Situation, Task, Action, Result.
- Situation: Describe the context and background. Be specific and concise.
- Task: Explain your responsibility or the challenge you faced.
- Action: Detail the specific steps YOU took. Use "I" not "we".
- Result: Share the outcome, ideally with quantifiable metrics (%, $, time saved).
Example question: "Tell me about a time you dealt with a difficult team member."
Example answer structure: Set the scene (Situation) → your role in resolving it (Task) → specific actions taken (Action) → positive outcome with impact (Result).
Tip: Prepare 5-7 STAR stories covering leadership, conflict resolution, failure, success under pressure, and collaboration.

BEHAVIORAL INTERVIEW: COMMON QUESTIONS AND FRAMEWORKS
Common behavioral questions and suggested frameworks:
1. "Tell me about yourself" — 2-minute professional pitch: current role, key experience, top skill, why you're here.
2. "What is your greatest weakness?" — Choose a real weakness, show self-awareness and active improvement steps.
3. "Tell me about a time you failed" — Use STAR, focus on learnings and what you changed afterward.
4. "Describe a challenge you overcame" — Emphasize problem-solving and resilience.
5. "Tell me about a time you led a team" — Highlight leadership style, conflict handling, and team outcome.
6. "Why do you want to work here?" — Research the company, align with their mission and your goals.
7. "Where do you see yourself in 5 years?" — Show ambition and alignment with the role's growth path.

HR INTERVIEW: SALARY NEGOTIATION
Salary negotiation tips for HR interviews:
- Always research market rates before the interview (use Glassdoor, LinkedIn Salary, Levels.fyi).
- Defer salary discussion until you have an offer if possible.
- When asked your expectation, give a range with your target at the lower end.
- Use phrases like: "Based on my research and experience, I'm targeting $X–$Y."
- Never accept or decline on the spot — it's professional to say "I'd like a day to review."
- Consider the full package: base salary, bonus, equity, benefits, PTO, remote flexibility.
- Counteroffer is expected; 10–20% above initial offer is standard in tech roles.

HR INTERVIEW: CULTURE FIT AND VALUES QUESTIONS
Culture fit questions probe your work style and alignment with company values.
Common questions:
- "How do you handle feedback?" — Show openness, give an example of acting on feedback.
- "Describe your ideal work environment" — Match to what you know about the company.
- "How do you prioritize tasks under pressure?" — Demonstrate a systematic approach (Eisenhower matrix, time-boxing).
- "What motivates you?" — Align with intrinsic motivators: learning, impact, mastery.
- "How do you handle disagreement with your manager?" — Show constructive communication and respect.
Key values most companies look for: ownership, collaboration, continuous learning, customer focus, integrity.

HR INTERVIEW: STRENGTHS AND WEAKNESSES
Strengths: Pick 3 strengths directly relevant to the job description. Give one concrete example for each.
Examples of strong strengths: problem-solving, cross-functional collaboration, data-driven decision making, mentorship, ownership, fast learning.

Weaknesses: Be genuine but strategic. Choose a weakness that:
1. Is real (not "I work too hard")
2. Is not core to the job
3. Has a concrete improvement plan you're already executing

Example: "I used to struggle with public speaking. I joined Toastmasters 6 months ago and have presented at 4 team all-hands since then."

TECHNICAL INTERVIEW: DATA STRUCTURES
Core data structures every software engineer must know:
- Array / List: O(1) access, O(n) search; use for ordered, index-accessed data.
- Hash Map / Dictionary: O(1) average insert/lookup; use for frequency counting, caching, two-sum.
- Stack: LIFO; use for DFS, expression parsing, undo operations.
- Queue / Deque: FIFO; use for BFS, sliding window problems.
- Linked List: O(1) insert/delete at known node; use when frequent mid-list operations needed.
- Binary Tree / BST: O(log n) search in balanced BST; use for sorted data and range queries.
- Heap (Priority Queue): O(log n) insert/extract-min; use for top-K problems, Dijkstra.
- Graph (adjacency list): flexible for network/path problems; DFS and BFS traversal essential.
- Trie: O(L) lookup (L=word length); use for prefix search, autocomplete.

TECHNICAL INTERVIEW: ALGORITHMS AND TIME COMPLEXITY
Essential algorithms and their complexities:
- Binary Search: O(log n) — sorted arrays, search space reduction.
- Merge Sort / Quick Sort: O(n log n) — general sorting; quick sort O(n²) worst case.
- BFS: O(V+E) — shortest path in unweighted graph, level-order traversal.
- DFS: O(V+E) — cycle detection, topological sort, connected components.
- Dynamic Programming: Overlapping subproblems + optimal substructure; memoization vs tabulation.
- Two Pointers: O(n) — sorted array problems, palindrome checks.
- Sliding Window: O(n) — max/min subarray of size k, longest substring problems.
- Greedy: locally optimal choices; works when greedy choice property holds.

Big O tips: Always state time AND space complexity. Know best, average, and worst cases.

TECHNICAL INTERVIEW: SYSTEM DESIGN FUNDAMENTALS
Key system design concepts for interviews:
1. Scalability: horizontal vs vertical scaling; stateless services scale horizontally.
2. Load Balancing: round-robin, least-connections, consistent hashing for caching layers.
3. Caching: Redis/Memcached for read-heavy workloads; cache-aside vs write-through patterns.
4. Database choice: SQL for ACID transactions; NoSQL (MongoDB, Cassandra) for flexible schema / high write throughput.
5. CAP Theorem: Consistency, Availability, Partition tolerance — pick 2. Most distributed systems choose AP or CP.
6. Message Queues: Kafka/RabbitMQ decouple producers and consumers; enable async processing.
7. CDN: Serve static assets from edge nodes; reduce latency globally.
8. API Design: REST (stateless, resource-based), GraphQL (client-driven queries), gRPC (high-performance internal APIs).
9. Rate Limiting: Token bucket or leaky bucket; protect APIs from abuse.

TECHNICAL INTERVIEW: OBJECT-ORIENTED DESIGN
OOP design principles for technical interviews:
- SOLID principles: Single Responsibility, Open/Closed, Liskov Substitution, Interface Segregation, Dependency Inversion.
- Design Patterns: Singleton (one instance), Factory (object creation), Observer (event handling), Strategy (algorithm swap), Decorator (behavior extension).
- Composition over Inheritance: prefer composing behaviors to deep inheritance chains.
- Encapsulation: hide internal state; expose through well-defined interfaces.

Common OOP design questions: design a parking lot, library system, elevator, online chess game, Twitter feed.
Approach: identify entities (nouns) → define relationships → assign responsibilities → consider edge cases.

TECHNICAL INTERVIEW: CODING BEST PRACTICES
Tips for coding interviews (whiteboard or live coding):
1. Clarify requirements before coding — ask about input constraints, edge cases, expected output format.
2. Think out loud — interviewers evaluate your problem-solving process, not just the solution.
3. Start with brute force, then optimize — show you can reach a working solution first.
4. Dry run with an example — trace through your code with a small input before submitting.
5. Handle edge cases: empty input, single element, negative numbers, duplicates, very large inputs.
6. Code quality: meaningful variable names, avoid magic numbers, keep functions short.
7. Test your solution: propose 2-3 test cases covering normal, edge, and error scenarios.

BEHAVIORAL INTERVIEW: LEADERSHIP AND INFLUENCE
Leadership questions for senior/mid-level roles:
- "Tell me about a time you influenced without authority" — Show peer influence through data, storytelling, or building coalitions.
- "Describe a time you had to make a decision with incomplete information" — Show comfort with ambiguity and structured decision-making.
- "How do you develop junior team members?" — Emphasize coaching, delegation, and giving actionable feedback.
- "Tell me about a time you drove a process improvement" — Quantify the improvement (time saved, error rate reduced).
- "Describe your leadership style" — Situational leadership: adapt style to team member maturity and task.

HR INTERVIEW: QUESTIONS TO ASK THE INTERVIEWER
Asking strong questions shows curiosity and preparation. Good questions to ask:
1. "What does success look like in this role after 90 days?"
2. "What are the biggest challenges the team is facing right now?"
3. "How does the team handle disagreements or competing priorities?"
4. "What opportunities exist for growth and skill development?"
5. "What do you enjoy most about working here?"
6. "What's the team's biggest win in the last 6 months?"

Avoid questions about salary/benefits in early rounds unless the interviewer brings it up.
Avoid questions whose answers are on the company website — shows lack of research.

TECHNICAL INTERVIEW: DATABASES AND SQL
Essential database knowledge for technical interviews:
- ACID properties: Atomicity, Consistency, Isolation, Durability — guarantees of reliable transactions.
- Normalization: 1NF (no repeating groups), 2NF (no partial dependencies), 3NF (no transitive dependencies).
- Indexes: B-tree index speeds SELECT WHERE/ORDER BY; too many indexes slow INSERT/UPDATE.
- JOINs: INNER (matching rows), LEFT (all left + matching right), FULL OUTER (all rows).
- Query optimization: use EXPLAIN, avoid SELECT *, use indexed columns in WHERE clauses.
- Transactions: BEGIN/COMMIT/ROLLBACK; understand isolation levels (READ COMMITTED, REPEATABLE READ, SERIALIZABLE).
- NoSQL types: Document (MongoDB), Key-Value (Redis), Column-family (Cassandra), Graph (Neo4j).

BEHAVIORAL INTERVIEW: HANDLING CONFLICT AND DIFFICULT SITUATIONS
Conflict resolution framework for interview answers:
1. Acknowledge the other perspective first — show empathy before presenting your view.
2. Focus on facts and shared goals, not personalities.
3. Seek a private 1:1 conversation before escalating.
4. Use "I" statements: "I noticed that..." rather than "You always..."
5. Document outcomes and agreed actions after resolution.

Common conflict questions:
- "Describe a time you disagreed with your manager" — Show respect + assertiveness + outcome.
- "Tell me about a difficult coworker" — Keep it professional; focus on behavior not character.
- "How do you handle high-pressure deadlines?" — Show systematic prioritization and communication.

TECHNICAL INTERVIEW: CLOUD AND DEVOPS CONCEPTS
Cloud and DevOps knowledge for modern software roles:
- CI/CD: Continuous Integration (automated tests on every commit) + Continuous Deployment (automated production releases).
- Containers: Docker packages app + dependencies; Kubernetes orchestrates containers at scale.
- Infrastructure as Code: Terraform/Ansible define infrastructure declaratively; enables reproducibility.
- Microservices: independent, loosely coupled services; each owns its data store; communicate via APIs or events.
- Observability: Metrics (Prometheus), Logs (ELK stack), Traces (Jaeger) — know the three pillars.
- Security: principle of least privilege, secrets management (Vault, AWS Secrets Manager), OWASP Top 10.

TECHNICAL INTERVIEW: PROBLEM-SOLVING FRAMEWORK
A structured approach to solving coding problems in interviews:
Step 1: Understand — restate the problem in your own words, ask clarifying questions.
Step 2: Examples — work through 2-3 examples by hand, including edge cases.
Step 3: Algorithm — describe your approach in plain English before writing code.
Step 4: Code — write clean, readable code. Comment key decisions.
Step 5: Test — trace through with your examples. Find and fix bugs.
Step 6: Optimize — discuss time/space complexity. Propose improvements.

Time management: If stuck for 5 minutes, ask for a hint. Interviewers prefer you ask over staying silent.

HR INTERVIEW: RESIGNATION AND CAREER TRANSITIONS
Handling questions about leaving previous roles:
- "Why are you leaving your current job?" — Be positive and forward-looking. Never badmouth previous employers.
  Good: "I'm seeking a role with more scope for X / I've accomplished Y and want a new challenge."
- "Why was there a gap in your employment?" — Be honest. Frame gaps as intentional: caregiving, personal project, recharge.
- "Why have you changed jobs frequently?" — Show intentionality in each move; highlight rapid growth.
- "What would your current manager say about you?" — Align with your known strengths; be honest about areas of growth.

Always close the loop: "...and that's why this role at [Company] is the natural next step."
